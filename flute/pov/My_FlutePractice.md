# My Flute Practice Routine

Flute practice links are [here](../Links_FlutePractice.md).

## Prerequisites

- The obvious: Flute | Music stand | Bottle/glass of water
- List of pieces to practice (repertoire): Arranged so you can count each practice session with tally marks (like 𝍸 𝍷)
- Metronome: Playing in time with a metronome is a reasonable substitute for playing in time with other musicians
- Timer: So you get a notification when you reach the end of your practice session (or your break)


## Practice

1. Typically 30-60m

2. Combine scales with technique
   - Practice long notes, stacatto, slurs, etc. as scales [less than 5m]
   - I don't normally practice fast scales, but I feel I should do this

3. Combine warm ups and repertoire
   - Use easier/simpler/older pieces from your practice list as warm ups [10m]

4. Repertoire
   - For a tricky piece, the steps below are typically carried out over many practice sessions
   - Start playing at a managable speed (slowly) without a metronome
   - Get the notes right first (even with pauses/hesitation)...
   - Then get the timing right...
   - Then play against a metronome at a speed where you can play the whole piece smoothly without any or many mistakes. Record this practice speed on your sheet music.
   - If you haven't been playing with slurs, ties, staccato, etc. add them now...
   - Then increase the metronome speed by 5-10% (until you can play the whole piece smoothly without any or many mistakes). Record this new practice speed on your sheet music.
   - If you haven't been playing with dynamics, add it now...
   - Every so often, ensure you can play the piece without a metronome
   - Identify difficult passages, highlight them and practice them separately


## Tips

- Document which pieces/parts/excerpts you have practiced by putting a tally mark (like 𝍸 𝍷) against the piece on the practice list.
- If you are struggling with a piece, it's becoming a chore and you've already spent a reasonable time on it this session (perhaps 15-20 minutes) then move on to another piece.
- If you can't read a particular section fast enough, consider memorising a few bars.
- If you cannot practice the whole piece in one practice session, try to budget enough time to practice the whole piece before the next lesson.
- Consider recording yourself
- Consider playing with accompaniment [recorded or with other musicians]
- Take breaks
- Drink water


## Goals worth noting (to help with motivation)

- When you reach your allocated practice time (the timer's alarm fires)
- When you can play a piece smoothly (without hesitation) with the metronome
- When you can play a piece with the metronome faster than a previous session
- When you can play a difficult passage that you couldn't play in a previous session
